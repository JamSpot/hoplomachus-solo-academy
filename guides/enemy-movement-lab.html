BOARD-STATE TACTICS

Enemy movement & ability lab

The goal is to make the enemy's next move understandable: inspect the actual board, apply its printed priorities and movement rules, then compare legal outcomes without replacing the official AI procedure with intuition.

Core principle: The optimum move for the enemy is not necessarily the move a human opponent would choose. First determine the move prescribed by the specific arena, mode, unit and ability text. Only then analyse tactical consequences. Official rulebooks and errata take precedence.

How each board analysis will work

  1. Identify the ruleset. Victorum, Remastered, mode, arena, expansion/module and any scenario-specific instructions.
  2. Read the enemy identity. Unit name, current health, movement, range, skills, status effects, controller and any special chip or tactic.
  3. Map the position. Record every occupied hex, blocked hex, relevant objective and each unit's movement/range. If a screenshot does not make a hex or chip readable, ask for a clearer image rather than guess.
  4. Apply enemy priority rules. Determine its highest-priority reachable mark using the arena/mode reference. Keep movement priorities distinct from attack-target priorities.
  5. List legal movement options. Account for movement allowance, occupied hexes, dazed/deployed restrictions, skill effects, range and any arena-specific movement rule.
  6. Resolve abilities at the correct timing. Check whether a skill is Innate, Ability or Special, and its timing, conditions, targets, limits and exceptions.
  7. Choose the rule-correct action. Follow deterministic priority text where it dictates the result. If the rules leave a genuine choice to the player, explain the available legal options and the tactical trade-offs.
  8. Forecast the consequences. Show likely threats, exposed units, objective pressure, attack opportunities and how the player can respond on their next turn.

Movement priority versus targeting priority

These are separate questions. A rival's movement behaviour determines the mark it tries to reach and where it moves; its attack behaviour determines which eligible unit it targets when engaging. A unit may move toward an objective or marked opponent yet have a different target-selection rule when it attacks. Each analysis must use the relevant printed priority list and current rules.

Abilities and special skills

Every skill database entry will record the exact skill name, skill category, timing window, trigger/condition, legal target, effect, restrictions, movement implications, interaction notes and official FAQ/errata. We will explicitly check abilities that move or displace units, alter movement or range, change targeting, prevent damage, retaliate, heal, or modify actions. A skill will never be assumed to activate outside its printed timing.

What the visual board review will show

  • Current board state and the relevant units/hexes.
  • Enemy's highest reachable priority and why higher priorities are unavailable.
  • Legal destination hexes, movement path and any blocked route.
  • Expected final position and attack range after moving.
  • Ability/special timing and whether its conditions are met.
  • Threat ranking and the strongest player response, including any unavoidable consequence.

Research coverage checklist

  • Victorum: rival movement, marking, targeting, deployment/dazed restrictions, abilities, specials, tactics, arena objectives, Primuses, Scions and expansion-specific priorities.
  • Remastered: AI troop activation, movement and target priorities, mode-specific AI, Titans, Immortals and arena-unit behaviour.
  • Shared skill library: every Innate, Ability and Special on relevant unit references, with official skill clarifications and errata.
  • Expansion audit: Pandora's Ruin, Pandora's Wake, Pandora's Might, The Forge, Mercury's Boots and official integration rules where applicable.
  • Worked examples: straightforward cases, blocked movement, competing priorities, ties, special abilities and edge cases.

Source and ruling standard

Use the official source register and the publisher's Hoplomachus support library. The published FAQ includes skill questions and expansion-specific errata, including examples where a rival's priority list is clarified. Every tricky interaction should be verified against current official material before being labelled definitive.
